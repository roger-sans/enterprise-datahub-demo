# Review walkthrough

1. **Introduce the release goal.** Open Platform overview. Explain which teams own the three products and why Finance and Operations apply different grains over the same dispatch events. Point out the quality alert created by the intentional failure if the demo was seeded with `--with-incident`.
2. **Take the consumer path.** Filter the catalogue to Finance and open Freight Spend. Select a region and month, apply the filters, then download CSV. Compare the row count, integer freight cents and product contract, and call out the publication ID in the preview and the CSV response header.
3. **Show traceability and reuse.** Open Lineage on Freight Spend. Follow Shipment Reliability from the shared-source link. Compare source SHA-256 and standardized event counts. Gold and Serving differ because the products answer different questions.
4. **Exercise a quality incident.** As Platform Operator, run Freight Spend with the negative monetary value scenario. The run is blocked at Silver, Gold does not materialise, and the accepted CSV and its publication ID are unchanged. Acknowledge the quality alert, start a standard refresh and confirm automatic resolution.
5. **Negotiate a contract change.** In Governance choose Shipment Reliability and switch to Fulfilment Data Owner. Try removing a published metric to see the compatibility finding. Submit the nullable annotation with a rationale. Switch to Governance Steward, record a review note and approve. The Contract screen shows the approved version alongside the published version. Switch to Platform Operator, run a standard Shipment Reliability refresh and confirm that v1.1.0 appears in the preview and CSV.
6. **Discuss delivery.** Open Delivery roadmap and explain the sequencing and trade-off in the Snowflake spike. A successful local export is evidence for a sandbox review. The next decision depends on measured parity, denial probes, recovery and actual credit use.
